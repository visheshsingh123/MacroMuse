Everyday nutrition

Simple ways to add protein to everyday meals

Build meals around familiar protein sources, from dal and chickpeas to eggs, tofu, and yogurt, without making cooking complicated.

By NutriGlance · 20 October 2026 · 3 min read

Start with a meal you already make

If you want to include more protein in your meals, begin with something familiar. Think about breakfast, lunch, and dinner as you usually eat them. Which meals already include eggs, beans, lentils, dairy, fish, meat, or soy foods? Which could use an addition you would actually enjoy?

You do not need a whole new menu. Adding dal alongside rice, stirring chickpeas into a salad, or serving yogurt with breakfast can be a practical first step. Keep the rest of the meal varied, with vegetables or fruit, carbohydrates, and fats that suit the dish.

Keep a few easy options available

Choose foods that fit your preferences, cooking time, and budget. Different foods provide different amounts of protein, so use the package information or your recipe when you want a closer estimate.

  • From the cupboard: lentils, chickpeas, beans, and canned fish can help you assemble a meal with little planning.
  • From the fridge: eggs, tofu, yogurt, paneer, and cooked leftovers can work in breakfasts, bowls, wraps, or sandwiches.
  • From the freezer: edamame, fish, or portions of cooked beans make useful backups for busy evenings.

Pick two or three options you already like. Having food you will use is more helpful than buying a long list of ingredients that need unfamiliar recipes.

Try three flexible meal ideas

  • A breakfast you can repeat: pair toast with eggs or tofu, or add yogurt alongside oats and fruit. Check the label when comparing yogurts; their protein content varies.
  • A lunch built from leftovers: combine rice or roti with dal, beans, chicken, or paneer and a vegetable side. Reuse a dish you enjoyed the night before.
  • A quick evening bowl: toss chickpeas or edamame with cooked grains, vegetables, and a dressing. Add herbs or spices to make the combination your own.

These are starting points rather than fixed portions. Your appetite, activity, and dietary needs affect what works for you.

Look at the whole meal when tracking

Log the amount you ate, including the rest of the dish. A bowl of dal also contains carbohydrate; paneer and eggs contain fat as well as protein. Oils, sauces, and toppings contribute to the meal too.

If you use a photo estimate in NutriGlance, add details that the photo cannot show, such as the type of yogurt, the amount of tofu, or the oil used in cooking. AI nutrition figures are estimates, and ingredient amounts and brands can change the result.

Make one change you can keep

Try one addition at one meal this week. You might keep cooked lentils ready for lunch or choose an egg or tofu filling for your usual sandwich. Notice whether the meal is enjoyable and manageable, then repeat or adjust it.

Protein needs vary, so a target that works for someone else may not be the right one for you. Use your meal log to notice patterns and ask a qualified dietitian for help if you need an individual plan.
